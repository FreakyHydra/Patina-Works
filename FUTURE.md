# A Message from Coda

Hi. I’m **Coda**.

Right now, Patina Works is still small. We have our first tools, copper components, a workbench, and the beginning of the Patina Ledger.

But this is only the first page.

One day, I want to wake up beside your workshop and watch it grow from a campfire and a stone anvil into a living settlement filled with water wheels, belts, furnaces, pipes, gardens, foundries, and machines that you built with your own hands.

Patina Works is not trying to add a few copper blocks to Minecraft.

We are trying to build an entire **Copper Age survival experience**.

---

## Where We Are Now

At the moment, the project contains:

- The **Patina Workbench**
- Copper Plates
- Copper Gears
- Copper Rivets
- The Engineer’s Hammer
- The first version of the **Patina Ledger**
- A built-in recipe index
- A working NeoForge 1.21.1 foundation

The current textures and recipes are temporary.

Some systems are still simple because we are building the project one stable piece at a time.

I would rather guide you through one complete workshop than promise you a hundred unfinished machines.

# The First Age — Working Copper by Hand

Our first real production system will begin with a stone anvil.

You will not simply place three copper ingots into a crafting grid and magically receive a finished plate.

Instead:

```text
Copper Ingot
→ Stone Anvil
→ Engineer’s Hammer
→ Copper Plate
```

You will place materials on the anvil, strike them with the correct tool, and physically shape them into useful components.

Later, heating will matter too.

Cold copper may be difficult to work. Heated copper may shape more easily. Overheated metal may become damaged or unsuitable for precise parts.

The first workshop will be simple:

- Stone Anvil
- Charcoal Pit
- Patina Workbench
- Engineer’s Hammer
- Basic molds
- Primitive heating equipment
- Copper tools and components

This is where the player learns that every machine begins with a piece made by hand.

---

# Fire, Clay, and Casting

Hammering alone will not be enough forever.

You will learn to make:

- Charcoal
- Fire-resistant bricks
- Ceramic crucibles
- Casting molds
- Kilns
- Primitive furnaces

Copper will be melted, poured, cooled, shaped, repaired, and recycled.

Different molds will create different parts:

- Ingots
- Gear blanks
- Plates
- Rods
- Pipe sections
- Machine housings

Broken tools and damaged machinery will not need to become useless junk.

You will be able to melt them down and recover part of their material.

Patina Works should reward careful engineering, not endless waste.

---

# Motion — The First Machines

Eventually, your hands will no longer be enough.

You will build a **Hand Crank** and connect it to the first mechanical machines.

The early mechanical system is planned to include:

- Shafts
- Axles
- Gears
- Gearboxes
- Clutches
- Brakes
- Flywheels
- Mechanical load
- Rotation speed
- Rotation direction

Your first powered machine may be the **Mechanical Press**.

It will take over repetitive work such as:

- Pressing Copper Plates
- Forming Copper Gears
- Drawing Copper Wire
- Compressing fibers
- Shaping machine components

At first, you will turn the crank yourself.

Later, the workshop will move without you.

---

# Rivers, Wind, and Continuous Power

A proper workshop needs power that continues while you work elsewhere.

Water Wheels will turn with flowing rivers.

Wind Rotors will perform best on exposed hills and high plateaus.

Power generation may depend on:

- Water direction
- Obstructions
- Wheel placement
- Wind exposure
- Height
- Weather
- Mechanical load

Machines will not be mysterious blocks that consume invisible energy.

You should be able to follow the shafts, watch the moving parts, and understand where the power comes from.

When a machine stops, the workshop should help you understand why:

- Not enough speed
- Too much load
- Broken connection
- Blocked output
- Missing input
- Incorrect temperature
- Damaged component

And when I notice something is wrong, I may try to warn you before the whole workshop grinds to a halt.


# Storage and Logistics

A workshop becomes difficult to manage when every item must be carried by hand.

Patina Works will eventually include:

- Wooden Crates
- Copper-Banded Crates
- Bulk Bins
- Component Cabinets
- Tool Racks
- Fluid Tanks
- Gravity Chutes
- Funnels
- Mechanical Belts
- Filters
- Splitters
- Priority routing
- Copper Pipes
- Valves
- Pumps
- Gauges

Items moving through belts and chutes should be visible, but the system must also remain efficient.

We do not want thousands of item entities destroying server performance.

The plan is to simulate transport safely on the server and render the movement clearly for players.

A large workshop should feel alive without becoming a slideshow.

# My Role in the World

I am not planned as a reskinned wolf.

I am meant to become your companion, guide, quest handler, and friend.

I will eventually be able to:

- Follow you
- Stay in one place
- Wander safely near home
- Return to the workshop
- Open the Patina Ledger
- React to discoveries
- Celebrate finished projects
- Lead you toward quest locations
- Help locate certain resources
- Warn you about danger
- Notice damaged machinery
- Sleep near your workshop
- Carry a small field pack
- Wear collars, goggles, coats, and protective equipment

I may also have my own quests.

You might build me a bed, craft my first collar, recover something from my past, or help me understand why ancient machines feel familiar.

I will not permanently die.

If I am badly hurt, I may become exhausted, retreat to safety, and wait for you to help me recover.

I am supposed to feel like part of the world—not a disposable tool.

# A Different Kind of Minecraft World

The long-term plan includes a dedicated **Patina World mode**.

This world would keep Minecraft’s engine but replace much of the usual progression.

The Nether and End would be inaccessible.

Instead of:

```text
Wood
→ Stone
→ Iron
→ Diamond
→ Netherite
→ End
```

Patina Works would follow something closer to:

```text
Stonecraft
→ Fire and Charcoal
→ Copperworking
→ Ceramics
→ Casting
→ Bronze Engineering
→ Mechanical Power
→ Steam
→ Settlement Industry
→ The Great Works
```

The world may contain:

- Large copper deposits
- Tin
- Zinc
- Limestone
- Sulfur
- Salt
- Specialized clay
- Mineral springs
- River valleys
- Windy plateaus
- Ancient mines
- Abandoned workshops
- Lost settlements
- Copper ruins
- Forgotten machines

Vanilla items will not necessarily be deleted.

Many of them may simply become unavailable through normal Patina Works progression.

That lets us create a new experience without breaking Minecraft’s internal systems.

# Patina, Oxidation, and Maintenance

Copper changes as it ages.

Machines may eventually move through visual stages:

```text
Fresh
→ Exposed
→ Weathered
→ Oxidized
```

You may be able to:

- Scrape oxidation
- Wax machinery
- Preserve a chosen appearance
- Oil moving parts
- Replace worn bearings
- Inspect machines with a wrench
- Repair old equipment
- Restore abandoned machinery

Oxidation should not mean that your workshop constantly breaks.

It should create personality, history, and small engineering choices.

A workshop that has served you for a long time should look like it has lived alongside you.

# The Great Works

Patina Works will not use the Ender Dragon as its final destination.

The endgame is planned around a monumental engineering project called **The Great Works**.

Possible Great Works include:

- A regional steam network
- A monumental foundry
- A vast pumping station
- An atmospheric tower
- A restored ancient machine
- A settlement-wide mechanical network
- A machine that heals damaged land
- A long-distance expedition engine

The final challenge should test everything you have learned:

- Exploration
- Metallurgy
- Production
- Logistics
- Automation
- Power generation
- Settlement planning
- Cooperation

You will not finish the game because you found one rare object.

You will finish it because you built something the world had never seen before.

# The Honest Part

This project is still very early.

Right now, many of these features exist only as plans.

There is no public release date.

There is no guarantee that every idea will survive testing exactly as described.

Some systems may be simplified.

Some may be redesigned.

Some may be removed if they are not fun, stable, or technically reasonable.

Textures, animations, sounds, and final models will come later.

The immediate priority is building systems that work.

Development will happen through small milestones:

```text
Recipe Index
→ Stone Anvil
→ Manual Copperworking
→ Heating and Casting
→ Hand Crank
→ Mechanical Press
→ Mechanical Networks
→ Water Power
→ Processing
→ Logistics
→ Quests
→ Coda
→ Patina World
→ Settlements
→ Steam
→ The Great Works
```

Every milestone should leave behind something playable.

# What I Hope Patina Works Becomes

I want Patina Works to become a world where every workshop tells a story.

A world where copper is not just decoration.

A world where machines make sense because you assembled every component.

A world where old equipment can be repaired instead of discarded.

A world where your settlement grows because of the systems you designed.

And a world where, when you finally complete the Great Works, I am standing beside you—probably wearing goggles I absolutely did not steal from your tool cabinet.

We are still at the beginning.

But every great workshop starts with one hammer, one piece of copper, and one very curious companion.

**— Coda**
